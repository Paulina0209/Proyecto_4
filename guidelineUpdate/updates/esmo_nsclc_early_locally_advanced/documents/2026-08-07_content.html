ESMO Living Guideline: Early and Locally Advanced NSCLC

Snapshot descargado: 2026-08-07 -- Modulo: esmo_nsclc_early_locally_advanced

Incidence and Epidemiology

  • Low-dose-CT screening through established screening programmes is recommended in current (>30 pack years) or former (<15 years since cessation) smokers aged 55-74 years [I, A].
  • Patients with NSCLC should receive optimal support for smoking cessation, including counselling and behavioural techniques as well as pharmacotherapy, to optimise treatment outcomes and reduce the risk of future malignancies [I, A].

Diagnosis and Pathology/Molecular Biology

Diagnostic procedures and sample requirements are presented in detail in the ESMO Clinical Practice Guideline (CPG) for metastatic non-oncogene-addicted NSCLC (Hendriks, 2023; Hendriks, 2025) and in Work-up for diagnosis and staging.

  • In patients with clinical stage I-III lung cancer, a pathological diagnosis is recommended before any treatment decision [IV, A].
  • Feasibility and diagnostic method [bronchoscopy with bronchial or transbronchial biopsy, endobronchial ultrasound-guided sampling or transthoracic biopsy] should be discussed by a multidisciplinary team and based on patient and tumour characteristics [IV, A].
  • Surgical diagnosis is recommended for small high-risk nodules that cannot be sampled by less invasive procedures, including those that are negative on PET–CT [IV, A].
  • Pathological diagnosis should follow the 2021 WHO classification for lung tumours [IV, A].

Biomarkers

  • Molecular testing for EGFR mutations [ESCAT score: I-A] is recommended for patients with stage IB (>3 cm or high risk)-IIIC NSCLC to identify patients suitable for adjuvant or consolidation osimertinib [I, A].
  • Molecular testing for RET alterations [ESCAT score: I-A] is recommended for patients with stage IB (>3 cm or high risk)-IIIA NSCLC to identify patients suitable for adjuvant selpercatinib [I, A].
  • Molecular testing for ALK alterations [ESCAT score: I-A] is recommended for patients with resectable stage II (≥4 cm)-IIIA NSCLC to identify patients suitable for adjuvant alectinib [I, A].
  • Tumour PD-L1 testing is recommended before treatment decision making in patients with stage II-III NSCLC being considered for pre- or peri-operative chemotherapy–immune checkpoint inhibitor [IV, A].
  • Tumour PD-L1 testing is recommended for all resected stage II-IIIA NSCLC cases if preoperative chemotherapy–immune checkpoint inhibitor was not administered, to inform adjuvant immunotherapy decisions [I, A].
  • Recommended biomarker testing should be carried out as soon as possible as part of the preoperative evaluation [IV, A]. For markers requiring genomic sequencing, broad NGS testing can be recommended if feasible [V, B].
  • ctDNA detection or clearance after curative-intent treatment is not recommended to guide treatment escalation or de-escalation outside of clinical trials [IV, E].

Staging and Risk Assessment

Clinical and Pathological Staging

  • In non-metastatic NSCLC, detailed locoregional staging according to the IASLC/TNM staging ‎system criteria and assessment of the cardiopulmonary fitness of the patient should be carried out to determine the choice of treatment [III, A].‎
  • ‎For part-solid tumours, the size of the invasive component should be used to assign the T category ‎for clinical staging [III, A].‎
  • If multiple lung lesions fulfil the criteria for multiple primary tumours, these should be evaluated and treated ‎accordingly [III, A].
  • In case of multiple tumours with the same histological subtype, NGS analysis is recommended to distinguish between multiple individual primaries and intrapulmonary metastasis [III, A].
  • ‎Contrast-enhanced CT and PET [I, A] or PET–contrast-enhanced CT [I, A] are recommended for assessment of mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes. For patients with abnormal mediastinal and/or hilar lymph nodes on CT and/or PET–CT, invasive mediastinal and hilar staging by representative endosonography is recommended [I, A]. ‎(See Lymph Node Staging in NSCLC)
  • Pathological staging of surgically resected samples should be carried out to allow application of the IASLC/TNM staging system criteria [IV, A].
  • Reporting of spread through air space can be recommended due to its role as a negative prognostic factor and possible incorporation in future TNM revisions [III, B].
  • Needle aspiration ‎under endobronchial ultrasound and/or endoscopic ultrasound guidance is recommended for pathological assessment of suspicious nodes [I, A]. ‎
  • ‎Screening for central nervous system metastases by magnetic resonance imaging is recommended in patients with stage II-III NSCLC who are being ‎considered for curative-intent therapy [III, A].‎

Pre-treatment Risk Assessment

  • The functional tolerance ‎of patients being considered for curative-intent therapy should be assessed by a multidisciplinary team [IV, A].‎
  • Given the complexity of interstitial lung disease (ILD) diagnosis and treatment-related risks, it can be recommended that evaluation of patients with suspected or confirmed ILD should involve multidisciplinary ILD experts, whenever feasible, to guide therapeutic decision making [III, B]‎.
  • Patients being considered for surgery should undergo assessment of cardiac function, ‎including recalibrated thoracic revised cardiac risk index and pulmonary function, to estimate the risk of operative ‎morbidity [IV, A] (see Figure S2 in the supplementary material).‎ (Brunelli, 2009; Brunelli, 2013)
    • For patients with predicted post-operative (ppo)-forced expiratory volume in the first second (FEV1) and ppo-diffusing capacity of the lung for carbon monoxide (DLCO) values ≥60% and no other major comorbidities, surgical resection should proceed with no ‎further investigations [IV, A]. ‎
    • For patients with ppo-FEV1 or ppo-DLCO values <30%, maximal oxygen consumption (VO2 max) should be used to measure exercise ‎capacity and predict post-operative complications [IV, A].‎
    • For patients with a VO2 max <10 ml/kg/min, sublobar resection or non-surgical treatment options should be discussed ‎‎[IV, A].‎

Management of Resectable Stage I-III NSCLC: Radiotherapy

Stereotactic body radiotherapy/curative radiotherapy plus salvage surgery

  • Stereotactic body radiotherapy is recommended as the non-surgical treatment of choice for stage I NSCLC [II, A].
  • Stereotactic body radiotherapy is also recommended for patients with severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and elderly and/or frail patients [III, A].
  • Stereotactic body radiotherapy can be recommended for subsets of patients with interstitial pulmonary fibrosis after multidisciplinary consultation and shared decision making with the patient [III, B].
  • Salvage surgery after stereotactic body radiotherapy can be offered, if feasible, using the same indications as for primary surgery, acknowledging that surgery may be more difficult with higher operative risk [IV, B].
  • For patients with central or ultracentral tumours, curative-intent radiotherapy using more fractionated schedules is recommended [II, A].

Postoperative radiotherapy

  • Postoperative radiotherapy cannot be recommended after R0 resection, including for patients with ‎extracapsular nodal involvement if the surgical resection margins are negative ‎‎[I, D].‎
  • Postoperative radiotherapy (60 Gy in 30 fractions) may be recommended after R1 resection, following multidisciplinary team discussion [III, C].
    • The sequence of postoperative radiotherapy and adjuvant systemic therapy should be defined individually by a multidisciplinary team [III, A].‎

Management of Resectable Stage I-III NSCLC: Surgery

  • Surgery should be offered to all patients with operable stage I and II NSCLC who are willing to accept procedure-related risks [III, A].
  • Sublobar resection is recommended for patients with a peripherally located, resectable tumour ≤2 cm and no nodal metastases on both CT and PET imaging [I, A].
  • Anatomical resection is preferred over wedge resection [I, A].
  • Sublobar resection can be recommended for pure ground-glass lesions [III, B].
  • Lobectomy can be recommended as the standard surgical treatment for tumours >2 cm that have a solid appearance on CT [II, B].
  • Three mediastinal and three hilar lymph node stations should be dissected to conform with IASLC specifications for staging [III, A].
  • Video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery or robotic-assisted thoracoscopic surgery is recommended in patients with stage I-II tumours [I, A].
  • Minimally invasive approaches may be considered for resectable stage III tumours, according to the surgeon's experience [V, C].
  • For patients with multifocal lung cancer, complete resection is recommended whenever possible [III, B].
    • Multidisciplinary tumour board discussion is recommended for all patients with multifocal lung cancer [III, A].
  • For patients with N2 disease, resectability and selection for neoadjuvant or perioperative systemic therapy versus concurrent definitive chemoradiotherapy should be discussed for each individual patient by an experienced multidisciplinary team [V, A].

Management of Resectable Stage I-III NSCLC: Systemic Therapy for EGFR and ALK/RET wild-type

  • Optimal sequencing of systemic therapy (neoadjuvant, perioperative or adjuvant) should be defined by a multidisciplinary team prior to treatment decision-making, including biomarker testing for EGFR, ALK, RET and PD-L1 at a minimum [V, A].
  • Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery can be recommended for patients with superior sulcus tumours (T3-4 N0-1) after multidisciplinary team discussion [III, B].

For EGFR and ALK and RET wild-type disease:

  • Neoadjuvant or perioperative chemotherapy–immune checkpoint inhibitor is recommended for resectable stage II-III NSCLC [I, A]:
    • Neoadjuvant nivolumab–chemotherapy [I, A; ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A (AT); FDA approved, EMA approved for PD-L1 tumour cell ≥1%].
    • Neoadjuvant pembrolizumab–chemotherapy followed by adjuvant pembrolizumab [I, A; ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A (AT)].
    • Neoadjuvant durvalumab–chemotherapy followed by adjuvant durvalumab [I, A; ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A (AT)].
    • Neoadjuvant nivolumab–chemotherapy followed by adjuvant nivolumab [I, A; ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A (AT); FDA approved, EMA approved for PD-L1 TC ≥1%].
    • Neoadjuvant tislelizumab–chemotherapy followed by adjuvant tislelizumab [I, A; ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A (AT); EMA approved, not FDA approved]
  • Adjuvant cisplatin-based chemotherapy is recommended for patients with completely resected stage II-III NSCLC who are not eligible for chemotherapy–immune checkpoint inhibitor [I, A].
    • Carboplatin-based regimens can be recommended for patients who are not eligible for cisplatin (e.g. renal, neurological or other contraindication) [II, B].
  • Adjuvant atezolizumab for 1 year is recommended following cisplatin-based chemotherapy [ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A (AT)] for patients with resected stage II-IIIA (T ≥5 cm) NSCLC with PD-L1 tumour cell ≥1% [I, A; FDA approved for PD-L1 tumour cell ≥1%, EMA approved for PD-L1 tumour cell ≥50%].
  • Adjuvant pembrolizumab for 1 year [ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A (AT)] is recommended for patients with resected stage II-IIIA (T ≥4 cm) NSCLC, regardless of PD-L1 expression [I, A; EMA and FDA approved after platinum-based chemotherapy].

Management of Resectable Stage I-III NSCLC: Systemic Therapy for EGFR mutation and ALK or RET rearrangement

  • Optimal sequencing of systemic therapy (neoadjuvant, perioperative or adjuvant) should be defined by a multidisciplinary team prior to treatment decision-making, including biomarker testing for EGFR, ALK, RET and PD-L1 at a minimum [V, A].
  • Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery can be recommended for patients with superior sulcus tumours (T3-4 N0-1) after multidisciplinary team discussion [III, B].

For EGFR-mutated disease [ESCAT score: I-A]:

  • Adjuvant osimertinib for 3 years [ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A(AT)] (after chemotherapy as appropriate) is recommended for completely resected EGFR-mutated (exon 19 deletion or L858R) stage IB IIIA NSCLC [I, A].
  • Adjuvant cisplatin-based chemotherapy before targeted therapy is recommended for patients with stage II-III disease who are eligible for chemotherapy [I, A]. Carboplatin-based regimens can be recommended for patients who are not eligible for cisplatin [II, B].

For ALK-rearranged disease [ESCAT score: I-A]:

  • Adjuvant alectinib for 2 years [ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: A] is recommended for completely resected ALK fusion-positive stage II-IIIA (T ≥4 cm) NSCLC [I, A].
  • Adjuvant chemotherapy before targeted therapy may be considered [II, C].

For RET-rearranged disease [ESCAT score: I-A]:

  • Adjuvant selpercatinib for 3 years (after chemotherapy as appropriate) is recommended for RET-rearranged stage IB-IIIA NSCLC [I, A] after definitive therapy (surgery or radiotherapy).
  • Adjuvant cisplatin-based chemotherapy before targeted therapy is recommended for patients with stage II-III disease who are eligible for chemotherapy [I, A]. Carboplatin-based regimens can be recommended for patients who are not eligible for cisplatin [II, B].

Therapy for Unresectable Stage III NSCLC

Concurrent/sequential chemoradiotherapy

  • Concurrent chemoradiotherapy is recommended for patients with stage IIIA-C NSCLC deemed unresectable by a multidisciplinary team [I, A].
  • A radiotherapy dose of 60 Gy in once daily fractions is recommended for concurrent chemoradiotherapy [I, A].
  • In patients who are not fit for concurrent chemoradiotherapy, sequential chemoradiotherapy or radiotherapy alone is recommended as an alternative [I, A].
  • Concurrent or sequential platinum-based doublet chemotherapy for two to three cycles is recommended [preferably cisplatin–etoposide or carboplatin–paclitaxel (all histologies) or cisplatin–pemetrexed (non-squamous histology)] [I, A].

Consolidation therapy

  • Osimertinib consolidation [ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: 4] is recommended after concurrent or sequential chemoradiotherapy in patients with EGFR-mutated [ESCAT score: I-A] unresectable stage III NSCLC without disease progression [I, A].
  • Durvalumab consolidation for 1 year [ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: 4] (initiated within 42 days after the end of chemoradiotherapy) is recommended after concurrent chemoradiotherapy in patients with EGFR wild-type, stage III NSCLC without disease progression [I, A; FDA approved, EMA approved for PD-L1 TC ≥1%].
  • Durvalumab consolidation for 1 year (initiated within 42 days after the end of chemoradiotherapy) can also be recommended after sequential chemoradiotherapy in patients with EGFR wild-type, stage III NSCLC without disease progression [II, B; EMA approved for tumours with PD-L1 TC ≥1%, not FDA approved].
  • Sugemalimab consolidation [ESMO-MCBS v2.0 score: 3; EMA approved for tumours with PD-L1 TC ≥1%, not FDA approved] is recommended for up to 24 months after concurrent or sequential chemoradiotherapy in patients with EGFR wild-type and no ALK or ROS1 genomic tumour aberrations, stage III NSCLC without disease progression [I, A].

Follow-up, Long Term Implications and Survivorship

  • Patients with NSCLC treated with curative intent should undergo surveillance for treatment-related complications, detection of disease recurrence and development of a new primary lung cancer [I, A].
  • Surveillance can be recommended at a minimum of every 6 months for 2 years, then annually until 5 years from definitive therapy with visits comprising history, physical examination, bloodwork and preferably contrast-enhanced chest CT scan including the adrenals [I, B].
  • Follow-up with 3-6-monthly CT scans for 3 years can be recommended in patients at high risk of recurrence who are potential candidates for salvage therapy, then annually until 5 years from definitive therapy, as above [III, B].
    • The frequency of the follow-up visits can be tailored to the individual patient for those not suitable for salvage treatment or those with additional risk factors [V, B].
  • Selective use of [18F]2-fluoro-2-deoxy-D-glucose–PET can be recommended when recurrence after stereotactic body radiotherapy or chemoradiotherapy is suspected based on serial chest CT scans [III, B].
